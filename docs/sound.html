Chapter 23

Sound

A sandbox can have sound: programs in it play through your Mac's speakers and, if you want, record from its microphone.

On this page
  1. Make one
  2. Inside the sandbox
  3. The microphone and macOS
  4. Across pause, sleep and hibernation
  5. Known limitations

Not in public releases yet. The releases on Homebrew and GitHub do not include sound: --sound says so. This page describes a build that carries the sound kernel (make release PUBLIC=0).

A sandbox can have sound: programs in it play through your Mac's speakers and, if you want, record from its microphone. It is a setting, sandbox.audio, off unless you choose it:

valuewhat the sandbox gets
off (the default)no sound device — exactly as every other sandbox
speakersthe Mac's speakers; its microphone records silence, and macOS is never asked about it
onthe Mac's speakers and its microphone, once macOS allows it

Make one#

In the dashboard: New sandbox › Bridges › Sound. In a project file: audio: on. To give every new sandbox sound: doz config set sandbox.audio speakers.

Sound is a device in the sandbox's virtual machine, so it is chosen when the sandbox is created. Afterwards a sandbox can move between speakers and on (doz config set --sandbox a1 sandbox.audio on) — from its next start; a wake keeps what it slept with — but not to or from off: for that, create a new sandbox.

A sandbox with sound boots its own Linux kernel: the same one every sandbox uses, with ALSA and the virtio sound driver added. It ships with doz (never downloaded), and doz doctor says whether this doz carries it. It is kata-containers' kernel 6.18.15-186 (kata 3.28.0: its config and patch, unchanged) with four options added — CONFIG_SOUND, CONFIG_SND, CONFIG_SND_VIRTIO, CONFIG_SND_PROC_FS — and nothing else; its recipe, the complete config and the source offer are in Dozable's source (Guest/sound-kernel/) and in a release's licences/sound-kernel/. It is built from source reproducibly — anyone can check it: make sound-kernel builds it (twice, in a throwaway Ubuntu sandbox) and compares its sha256 with the one doz pins.

Inside the sandbox#

Dozable's managed tools (Managed tools) sets a sandbox with sound up at every start and wake: ALSA's tools (aplay, arecord), an /etc/asound.conf that makes the sound card ALSA's default (one program at a time can play, and one can record — a second gets "Device or resource busy"), and doz-sound:

commanddoes
doz-sound infothe sound card, its devices, the default, and the microphone (the Mac's, or why it is silent)
doz-sound testplays a quiet tone (under a second), records 3 seconds, says the level, plays it back
doz-sound rec FILE [SECONDS]records a WAV file (5 seconds unless you say)
doz-sound play FILEplays a WAV file
doz-sound tone FILEwrites a quiet test tone (a WAV, under a second) to play

Any program that uses ALSA's default device works the same way.

The microphone and macOS#

macOS asks about the microphone for the app that started doz — Terminal, iTerm, your editor — not for doz itself. It asks once; after that its answer stands until you change it in System Settings › Privacy & Security › Microphone.

A sandbox records from the Mac's microphone only if macOS allowed it before the sandbox started. The microphone is part of the sandbox's virtual machine, fixed when it starts and kept through Pause, Sleep and Hibernate (a wake keeps the microphone it went to sleep with). Otherwise — not asked yet, denied, or the question left unanswered — the sandbox records silence, and doz-sound says why.

When doz asks is a setting, sandbox.microphone_ask:

valuedoz asks macOSthen
first_recording (the default)when a program in a sandbox with on first recordsthat recording is silent: once you allow it, Shut Down and Start the sandbox — doz says so in the sandbox's terminals and on the dashboard
first_startwhen a sandbox with on startsthe start waits for your answer (at most a minute), so the first recording already has the microphone

With first_recording, a sandbox whose sound is on starts with a silent microphone while macOS has no answer, and doz watches it for a program that records (a recording stream of its sound card that runs — arecord, doz-sound, any ALSA program). The first one makes doz ask: macOS shows its question ("Terminal would like to access the microphone"), and doz says in the sandbox's terminals and on the dashboard that this recording is silent and that, once you allow it, Shut Down and Start makes the sandbox record from the Mac. Nothing is asked again once macOS has answered.

doz doctor says macOS's answer, which app it is for and when doz asks; the sandbox's Home in the dashboard says what its microphone is.

Because nothing records from the Mac's microphone until macOS has said yes, pausing, sleeping or hibernating a sandbox is always safe, whatever macOS's answer.

Across pause, sleep and hibernation#

Sound keeps going across Pause, Sleep and Hibernate, and after the host restarted: a program that was playing or recording when the sandbox went to sleep carries on when it wakes, with nothing to restart.

Known limitations#

  • A recording from the Mac's microphone can start with a short stretch of silence and contain a few single-sample ticks (doz-sound leaves them out of its level).
  • Sound takes a moment to reach the speakers: with the standard tools' defaults, about three quarters of a second from playing a sound to hearing it back through the microphone; programs that use small buffers are much quicker.
  • A sandbox with sound wakes from hibernation a little more slowly (about 0.07 s).
  • One player and one recorder at a time.
  • Codex's voice mode does not work in a sandbox yet: it sends its sound over the network as UDP (WebRTC), which a proxied sandbox does not carry.
