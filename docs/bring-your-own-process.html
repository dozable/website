Bring your own process: a worked example

Dozable does not come with a process. It gives you the parts — a workspace with a brief, a room, agents, units of work (runs) and a journal for each, lessons that carry into the next — and you decide how they are used.

Dozable does not come with a process. It gives you the parts — a workspace with a brief, a room, agents, units of work (runs) and a journal for each, lessons that carry into the next — and you decide how they are used. This page walks through one real shape of process from start to finish: draft → render a still → review → revise, over three units of work, with two agents in one room and you deciding. Then it shows how to map a workflow you already have onto the same parts.

Read Runs, the journal and lessons for what each command does; this page shows them working together.

On this page: The set-up · The process, in the brief · The first unit of work · Finishing and the lesson · The second and third · Reading it afterwards · Your own workflow

The set-up#

The work: a short promotional video made with Remotion, one scene per unit of work. Two workers do it: bot1 drafts a scene and renders a still of it; bot2 reviews the still against criteria you set. You have no manager here — you drive, and you decide.

The dry run says what it would make — the workspace, its room promo, two declared workers in Sandboxes (the preset), the standard network and remotion.media — and anything that would be refused. Then, in this order — make it, open the first unit of work and set what "done" means, start the workers, grant them — so no worker ever looks at a unit of work with no criteria:

A declared worker becomes an agent of the workspace when it starts, so it is granted right after the start (doz grant before it refuses: the workspace has no agent of that name yet). Workers may read the journal from the start; writing needs the grant (reviewer is the usual one — What agents may write). An entry written in the moment before it is refused with exit 4, naming journal.write, and the agent tries again once you have granted it — nothing is lost.

The network. remotion-video gives its Sandboxes the standard network and remotion.media, where a render first downloads Remotion's own browser. Anything else a composition fetches (a web font, a remote image) is refused until you allow it — look, then allow exactly what it shows:

The process, in the brief#

The process is prose in the brief. Every agent reads it in the room at the start of each unit of work, so it is the one place to say who does what, in which order, and what each step writes in the journal:

Each worker was also told its own task when it was declared; the brief says how the two fit together.

bot1 hears the decision (dozchat ask-show promo #4 --wait 60 gives it as JSON) and goes on. An Ask nobody answers expires — in 24 hours unless it says sooner — and the answer is then decline, so nothing waits for ever.

Finishing and the lesson#

Finish closes the unit of work: its room is closed to new messages, the workers' sessions end with their conversations kept, and its evidence is archived in the store — the room's messages, each agent's conversation and last screen, the journal and the stills attached to it.

bot2's proposal waits as an Ask in the room: "Apply this lesson to the work description of promo?". Answer Apply there, or in a terminal:

You can also propose one yourself, from what you saw: doz workspace retro promo --propose --addendum "…". A lesson applied becomes an addendum of the work description.

The second and third units of work#

run opens the next unit of work and starts bot1 and bot2 again, in their own conversations. Its opening message in the room is the brief, then Lessons to apply: with the lesson from scene 1, then This run's goal: Scene 2: the product shot. So bot1 checks the title at phone size before it asks for a review, without anyone saying so again.

Set this scene's criteria, and the loop runs as before: task, still, review, revise, outcome. Finish it, decide any new lesson, and the third is the same:

Each unit of work carries every lesson approved before it. doz workspace run promo … --json says how many it carried (lessons).

Reading it afterwards#

A journal is the record of what happened, in order and by whom: each review against its criterion, each finding, each draft's still with its hash, the outcome. Nothing in it is edited; a correction is a new entry that supersedes the old.

Mapping your own workflow#

Dozable imposes no process: the same parts fit the one you already have. Write it in the brief, as above, and decide which journal kinds stand for its steps.

in your workflowin Dozable
a ticket or issuea unit of work, its goal the ticket (doz workspace run W "…" --source given); a task entry with --ref url:… to the ticket
the definition of donecriterion entries at the start of the unit of work (check can hold the command your process runs; Dozable never runs it)
a branch and a pull requestthe agent's own git, in the workspace's folder; a note with --ref commit:SHA and --ref url:… to the pull request
a code reviewreview entries against each criterion (subject the commit reviewed), finding entries for the problems
the tests or CI passinga note with the log attached (--attach), or a review whose criterion is the test run
a sign-offan outcome entry (accepted, accepted-with-issues, rejected), or an Ask to you in the room
a retrospectivedoz workspace retro W --propose --addendum "…", decided by you; applied lessons carry into every later unit of work
your team's own stepsyour own kinds, prefixed: acme.deploy, acme.release-note

Who may write what is yours to grant: a reviewer may write the journal and propose lessons (reviewer), deciding lessons is description.decide, opening and finishing units of work run.open and run.finish — each off for an agent until you grant it. A manager agent can run the loop for you instead of you driving it: make it with --manager BOT, and grant it what the process needs (Workspaces).
